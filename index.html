About

I am an atmospheric scientist working at the intersection of circulation dynamics, climate extremes, and Earth system modelling.

I completed my PhD in Geophysics at Victoria University of Wellington, New Zealand, in 2026, working with Prof. James Renwick.

My doctoral research, “Tipping the scales: The butterfly effect in Atmospheric Blocking and Temperature Extremes,” examined how atmospheric blocking and internal variability influence temperature extremes. I combine large climate-model ensembles with reanalysis data, circulation diagnostics, and statistical analysis to distinguish robust signals from the wide range of plausible atmospheric outcomes.

02

Research themes

Questions that guide my current and future work.

A

Atmospheric Blocking & Circulation Dynamics

I investigate atmospheric blocking, jet-stream behaviour, and persistent circulation regimes, with a focus on how their representation and inferred trends depend on model performance and diagnostic choices.

B

Climate Extremes & Internal Variability

I use large ensembles to quantify how internal variability shapes heat and cold extremes, conditional risk, and the spread of climate outcomes under common external forcing.

C

Predictability, Rare Events & Uncertainty

I am interested in atmospheric sensitivity, divergent ensemble trajectories, and circulation precursors—with the goal of improving predictability and characterising rare but consequential events.

Featured research · Geophysical Research Letters · 2026

How detection choices shape our picture of atmospheric blocking

Shelke, P. B. & Renwick, J. A. (2026). “Southern Hemispheric atmospheric blocking patterns in CESM-LENS2: Effects of Reversal versus Anomaly approaches.” Geophysical Research Letters, 53, e2025GL120428.

Using CESM2-LENS2, this work shows that Southern Hemisphere blocking climatology can depend strongly on whether blocking is detected using reversal- or anomaly-based methods. In contrast, some projected changes remain more consistent across approaches—clarifying which conclusions are method-sensitive and which are more robust.

Looking ahead

Future research

I aim to investigate rare extreme-event dynamics through large and branching ensembles, tracing how small differences in circulation evolve into divergent high-impact outcomes. This work will connect circulation sensitivity, internal variability, and predictability to more robust assessments of future climate risk.
