About Me

Just a curious climate scientist!

I completed my PhD (Geophysics) at Victoria University of Wellington, New Zealand, working with an amazing supervisor Prof. James Renwick. My thesis, Tipping the scales: The butterfly effect in Atmospheric Blocking and Temperature Extremes, examined how blocking, internal variability, and circulation uncertainty shape extreme-temperature risk under warming climate.

My research combines developing new methods, weather/climate model simulations, large climate-model ensemble assessment, circulation diagnostics, statistical analysis, and high-performance computing. A central theme is separating robust dynamical signals from outcomes that depend strongly on detection choices or internal variability (The so called 'butterfly effect').

My broader background and diverse research portfolio spans ocean–atmosphere interaction, climate teleconnections, Indian monsoon predictability, air–sea exchange modelling (physical and chemical), numerical/earth system modelling and climate model evaluation. I am especially interested in connecting atmospheric/ocean dynamics to predictability and decision relevant climate risk.

Research Themes

The scientific questions connecting my past work, current projects, and potential future research interests.

A

Atmospheric Blocking & Large-Scale Circulation Dynamics

I investigate blocking, jet stream behaviour, Rossby wave patterns and persistent circulation regimes, including how diagnostic choices affect their inferred climatology and future change.

C

Climate Extremes & Role of Internal Variability

I use climate models and large ensembles to quantify risks due to temperature extremes, compound blocked extremes, role of internal variability, and the spread of plausible climate outcomes under common forcing.

P

Predictability, Rare Events & Uncertainty

I study atmospheric sensitivity, confidence in projected change, divergent ensemble trajectories, and dynamical precursors that can help explain why rare high-impact events emerge.

O

Ocean–Atmosphere Interactions & Monsoon Variability

My earlier work examined Pacific Ocean temperature signals, Indian summer monsoon predictability, and oceanic teleconnections associated with drought and regional climate variability across Northern Hemisphere.

E

Earth System Modelling & Process Diagnostics

Across global, regional and process-oriented modelling, I am interested in physically interpretable diagnostics that connect model behaviour to circulation, air–sea exchange and environmental impacts (including recent developments in AI/ML techniques).

Research Projects & Scientific Contributions

Recently Published · Article

How detection choices shape interpretation of Southern Hemisphere atmospheric blocking

Question. How strongly does the diagnosed blocking climatology depend on whether blocking is defined by flow reversal or by persistent geopotential height anomaly?

Contribution. Demonstrated that Southern Hemispheric blocking detection method sensitivity is itself a major observational and methodological constraint, while identifying aspects of projected future change.

Geophysical Research Letters (2026) ↗

Current · Detection-method development

A novel Southern Hemispheric atmospheric blocking index

Question. Can a blocking diagnostic be designed around the distinctive stronger zonal flow, oceanic midlatitudes and circumpolar storm-track dynamics of the Southern Hemisphere?

Contribution. Developed a novel hemisphere-aware framework that combines anomaly, gradient, and persistence constraints for robust blocking detection.

Status: Manuscript close to submission.

Current · Northern Hemispheric blocking dynamics

Evaluating impacts of massive Greenland Blocking Decline in CESM-LENS2

Question. How does CESM-LENS2 represent Northern Hemisphere blocking, and what circulation structures help explain regional model bias and projected change?

Approach. Used hybrid blocking detection across the large ensemble, combined with circulation and jet-stream diagnostics for bias attribution.

Status: Manuscript in preparation.

Current · High-impact extremes

The butterfly effect in blocking and temperature extremes

Question. Does blocked-heat extreme risk remain or increase even where blocking frequency decline in future?

Contribution. Separating circulation/frequency effects from conditional risk effects to show how dynamics and a warmer thermodynamic background jointly shape future heat and cold extremes.

Status: Manuscript close to submission.

Climate risk mapping · Robustness & confidence

Global confidence maps for blocking and associated compound extremes

Question. Where are projected changes distinguishable from the large spread generated by internal variability?

Contribution. Developed a unique ensemble-based confidence information for blocking, heat waves, cold waves and co-located blocked extremes, including spatial significance and false-discovery-rate control.

Strong Value: A reusable Global uncertainty framework for circulation and compound-extreme analysis.

Master's & IIT Bombay research · Ocean–atmosphere dynamics

Pacific Ocean signals, Indian monsoon predictability & drought teleconnections

Question. How can ocean thermal variability and remote oceanic teleconnections inform Indian summer monsoon and drought variability?

Background. My master's thesis examined Indian summer monsoon predictability using Pacific Ocean mean temperature, followed by climate risk assessment, mitigation and adaptation related research at IIT Bombay on oceanic teleconnections and Indian drought.

Theme: Ocean–atmosphere coupling, monsoon variability and climate predictability.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie research · Mediterranean sea

Regional atmospheric modelling and air–sea exchange in the Mediterranean

Focus. Atmospheric and environmental modelling within an international research network, including regional modelling and pollution/dispersion processes over the Mediterranean.

Broader value. This work expanded my experience into regional atmosphere–ocean process modelling (including model development) and environmental applications.

Future Research direction · Understanding Our Changing Climate

Branching ensembles for dynamical leverage points in rare extremes - current working proposal

Research direction. Use targeted branching ensembles around high-impact events to identify when small circulation differences amplify into divergent outcomes.

Proposed framework. Branch before an event, diagnose divergence in blocking, jets and Rossby-wave structure, then re-branch around candidate dynamical leverage points.

Goal: Connect dynamical meteorology, large ensembles and interpretable data-driven diagnostics for rare-event predictability. I am working on turning more amazing ideas into research proposals across diverse research themes. Get in touch to know more.
